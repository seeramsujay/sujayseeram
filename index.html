DUST-VISION // IIT MADRAS SHAASTRA HACKATHON (2ND PLACE) 🏆

My first ever hackathon. IIT Madras. Shaastra Smart City Challenge.

I honestly didn't know what to expect walking in. First year. No senior to shadow. Just a problem statement and a laptop....

unfold_more Read Full Log
I honestly didn't know what to expect walking in. First year. No senior to shadow. Just a problem statement and a laptop.

We built `dust-vision` — a system to monitor and manage construction site dust emissions in real time using computer vision. The idea was simple: smart cities need air quality data *at the source*, not from a distant sensor 3 km away.

We came **2nd place**.

I remember the moment the results were announced and genuinely couldn't believe it. That one result made me want to do more hackathons, more projects, more of *this*.

First hackathon. Silver. Not a bad start. 🚀

GARC CHENNAI // AUTOMOTIVE EXPOSURE (EVENT)

Not every learning experience comes from a screen.

I attended the GARC (Global Automotive Research Competition) meet in Chennai, and it completely changed how I think about autonomous systems....

unfold_more Read Full Log
I attended the GARC (Global Automotive Research Competition) meet in Chennai, and it completely changed how I think about autonomous systems.

We got hands-on exposure to:
- **ADAS** (Advanced Driver Assistance Systems)
- **LIDAR sensors** and how they're used in real vehicles
- **CARLA** — the simulator used for training self-driving cars
- **Baja SAE** — engineering race cars from scratch

Hearing from people actually *building* this stuff is different. You stop treating autonomous vehicles as a concept and start seeing the engineering decisions behind every component.

That trip planted seeds that would later grow into `ADAS-Sensor`.
#GARC #AutonomousVehicles #LIDAR #ADAS #Engineering #Learning

Motorstone Hackathon. Solo.

unfold_more Read Full Log
The project: `motor-safe` — detecting and mitigating dangerous vibrations in motors before they cause mechanical failure.

Motors vibrate. That's normal. But when the frequency signature shifts — that's the warning sign most systems miss. We built something that listens.

This was one of those hackathons where I genuinely learned more from the process than the result. Real hardware, real sensor data, real-time processing. No fake datasets.

Walked away with a participation prize and a lot of respect for embedded systems work.
